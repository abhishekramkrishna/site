“Who owned this account on March 10?” and “Who is our contact today?” can both be easy questions. Put an ownership change between them and an agent can answer one of them very confidently with the wrong name.

There are two dates I’d keep explicit in semantic memory:

- When the ownership change took effect.
- When the system learned about it.

Suppose Noor took over on March 12 and the CRM correction arrived on March 16. In an audit of what the agent knew on March 14, I’d replay the earlier CRM view. In a retrospective of who actually owned the account on March 14, I’d use the corrected timeline.

That distinction matters in renewals, customer handoffs, and escalations. A late correction can change the historical answer without changing what the system could have known at the time.

Before buying or building temporal memory, I’d put one late correction in the eval set. Ask both questions and require the agent to show its source.

### Model the correction as two histories

Here’s the same effective date viewed from two different knowledge cutoffs.

| Question | Effective date | Information cutoff | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who did the system believe owned the account on March 14? | March 14 | March 14 | Riya |
| Who did the latest corrected history say owned it on March 14? | March 14 | March 17 | Noor |

I’d represent one interval for when a claim is effective and another for when that row was accepted as the system’s view. On March 16, close the earlier system-time interval. Add the two rows that express the corrected history: Riya up to March 12 and Noor starting March 12. Retain the CRM reference that justified the update. Half-open intervals make boundary behavior explicit when one owner’s term starts exactly when another ends.

The query filters on both intervals. Asking for the current contact sets both dates to the present. An audit of a past run uses the recorded cutoff of that run. A corrected historical answer uses the target effective date with a later cutoff.

### Spend on time semantics where they change an outcome

Many products can start with a current record plus a change log. Add explicit effective time when retroactive corrections, future-dated changes, or historical decisions matter to customers. Test scheduled handoffs, late-arriving events, overlapping records and an account with missing ownership. An overlap between equally authoritative sources should surface a conflict for a human to resolve.

For an audit, also capture the evidence or version that actually reached the agent. The CRM may have known about the correction before the agent retrieved it. These two facts answer different questions about the rollout.

**Try it.** Change only the information cutoff in the demo. Both answers should cite the version that was visible at the requested cutoff.

**Run the demo.** The fixture is [`examples/memory_demos.py`](https://github.com/abhishekramkrishna/agent-harness-lab/blob/main/examples/memory_demos.py) in agent-harness-lab. It runs offline, with no model or network calls, and asserts the behaviour described above.

## Conclusion

Use two clocks when knowledge and reality can diverge. Test the same effective day at different knowledge cutoffs and require the evidence that was visible at each cutoff.
